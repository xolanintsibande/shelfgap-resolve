# Risks, Assumptions and Open Questions

## Assumptions (not findings)
- Shelf gaps create enough recurring work to justify a structured workflow.
- Staff can access at least some relevant stock/order/delivery information.
- A guided checklist can reduce missed checks.
- Case ownership and follow-up are sometimes unclear.
- A separate product could complement existing systems.

## Product risks
- Duplicate functionality in existing retailer systems.
- Additional admin burden.
- Poor or delayed source data.
- Incorrect suggested checks.
- Users treat provisional causes as confirmed.
- False closure without follow-up.
- Low user adoption.
- Privacy, security or access restrictions.
- Dashboard metrics mislead when sample sizes are small.

## Open questions
1. How are shelf gaps currently reported?
2. Which role owns investigation and resolution?
3. What tools already exist?
4. Which fields are available to frontline users?
5. What is the current median time to resolution?
6. How often are gaps repeated at the same SKU-location?
7. What counts as a verified resolution?
8. What is the maximum acceptable time to create a case?
9. Who would pay for or sponsor the product, if value is validated?
10. What retailer security and procurement requirements would apply?

## Decision guardrails
- Do not claim demand without user discovery.
- Do not claim root causes without evidence.
- Do not claim commercial impact without measurement.
- Do not use real store or employer data in public demos without explicit authorisation.
